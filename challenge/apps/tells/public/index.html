    iPhone share sheet (any app)

    A Shortcut called Check with Tells takes text, links and pictures from the share sheet in any app.

    Build the Shortcut (5 minutes)
    1. In Shortcuts, tap + and name it Check with Tells. Under ⓘ, turn on Show in Share Sheet; let it receive Images, Safari web pages, Text and URLs.
    2. Add Get Images from Input, then If Images has any value.
    3. Inside the If: Base64 Encode the Images (call it Original). Convert Image to JPEG with Preserve Metadata off, Resize Image to width 1024, Base64 Encode it (call it Preview).
    4. Then Get Contents of URL: https://challenge.strongtechnicalconsulting.com/tells/api/check/picture?format=text, Method POST, Header Authorization = Bearer + a space + your device key (Settings → Device keys). Request Body JSON: original → Dictionary with data = Original; preview → Dictionary with data = Preview. Then Show Result.
    5. Otherwise (text and links): URL Encode the Shortcut Input, put it after https://challenge.strongtechnicalconsulting.com/tells/?src=shortcut&q= in a Text action, then Open URLs.

    Pictures are checked straight away and use credit (a visual read, about half a cent); their metadata is read free from the original on our server and nothing is kept. Text and links only open Tells with the free quick scan.

    Chrome extension

    A Tells button on every LinkedIn and X post, plus “Check selected text / this image / this page” on the right-click menu anywhere.

    Download the extension (.zip)

    Install it (“Load unpacked”)
    1. Unzip the file somewhere you will keep it.
    2. In Chrome, open chrome://extensions and turn on Developer mode (top right).
    3. Click Load unpacked and choose the unzipped folder.
    4. Open the extension’s Options, paste a device key from Settings → Device keys, and Save.

    It is not in the Chrome Web Store yet. It reads only the post you click and what you right-click; your device key stays in the extension.

    How Tells reads

    • Quick scan (free, instant, on your device): rules for the tells templated and model-written text over-use, each highlighted with its reason. Always low confidence.
    • Deep read (uses credit): a model reads the text for tells and human signs. Every highlight it gives is checked against your text; any quote that is not in it is thrown away.
    • Metadata (free, on your device): Content Credentials (C2PA), IPTC source type, generator settings and camera EXIF. The one strong signal, when it names an AI generator. We read Content Credentials but do not verify their signatures; verify them here. Most sites strip metadata, so finding none means nothing.
    • Visual read (uses credit): a model looks at the picture or 6 frames for artefacts. A weak signal.
    • Originality (uses credit and web search): earlier sources that share the idea or the wording, with links, and what the piece adds. Scored separately from the AI likelihood.

    Why no verdict? AI-text detection is unreliable. It wrongly flags human writing, especially people writing in a second language and polished professional prose, and light editing hides AI text. A number without its reasons would be an accusation. So Tells shows the evidence and how much weight it can bear.

    What is kept: nothing you check. No text, link, picture, frame or result is stored or logged. Pictures and frames are sent to Anthropic once for the read and dropped. Your recent checks list lives only in this browser. There are no share links: a public “this post is 87% AI” page would be aimed at a real person.
