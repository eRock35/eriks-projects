Chapter

A book club that keeps up, and never spoils the ending.

Every club has the same three problems: nobody knows who has read how far, the fast reader spoils it in the group chat, and “what next?” becomes a forty-message thread. Chapter handles all three. Friends join with a six-letter code and a first name, with no account and nothing to install.

  • A progress board. Everyone’s bookmark along the book, with the next meeting as the finish line and a nudge when you fall behind.
  • Spoiler-safe notes. Notes are tagged by chapter and stay locked until you have read that far. The server never sends a locked note’s words.
  • The vote for the next book. Nominate, then tap every book you would read. Counts stay hidden until the host closes the vote, and the tie-break is shown.
  • Meetups. A date, a place or a call link, RSVPs and a calendar file.
  • My library. Your own books across every club, added by scanning the barcode or typing the ISBN, and seen by nobody else.

    About

I work with C-suite leaders, business-unit presidents and account executives, internally and externally, to tell the story of how our products fit into each customer's ecosystem: the value we can add, and the value we've already delivered.

    I'm leading the organization's move to applied and agentic AI. The agents we've put into production save more than 1,700 hours a week: a research agent that investigates potential issues, and an insight agent that gives more than 100 account executives customer-ready talking points. That work, and the customer insights behind it, has driven new revenue and protected existing revenue, together more than $50 million, and earned the 2025 CEO Circle of Excellence award for pioneering and championing AI within EWS.

    I'm a coach player. I've led global teams across the US, Costa Rica and India, and I still build alongside them.

Along the way I built the insights and the algorithm that detect synthetic fraud in the credit marketplace, which became two patents, and I've won the company's top awards more than once, which is rare for someone who isn't in sales.
